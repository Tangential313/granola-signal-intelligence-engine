# Granola Company Overview

> Independent portfolio research for the Granola Signal Intelligence Engine. This project is not affiliated with or endorsed by Granola.

## Product

Granola is an AI-powered notepad for meetings. It combines a user's own notes with meeting transcription to create structured post-meeting notes, while also allowing users and teams to search, chat with and reuse context from previous conversations.

Unlike bot-based meeting assistants, Granola runs on the user's device and captures device audio rather than joining the meeting as a visible participant. Its product has expanded beyond individual note-taking toward a shared team workspace and organisational context layer.

## ICP and Users

Granola serves both individual professionals and teams, with a commercial path spanning free users, small teams, mid-market organisations and enterprise customers.

Its current product and hiring signals suggest particular relevance for knowledge workers in meeting-heavy roles and for organisations that want to turn meeting context into shared, searchable institutional knowledge.

## Pricing and Business Model

Granola currently offers three main tiers:

- **Basic:** free, with limited meeting history.
- **Business:** $14 per user/month, with unlimited history, advanced AI features, integrations and MCP access.
- **Enterprise:** $35+ per user/month, adding enterprise security, administration and organisational controls.

This creates a natural product-led expansion path from individual adoption to team and enterprise deployment.

## Positioning

Granola positions itself as more than a traditional AI meeting recorder. What began as an AI notepad for back-to-back meetings is increasingly described as a shared team workspace and a "second brain" for organisational context.

The strategic direction is notable: instead of treating transcripts as an end product, Granola is positioning conversation data as reusable context that can support downstream workflows, search and AI-enabled work across an organisation.

## Competitive Landscape

Relevant competitors and adjacent products include:

- Fathom
- Fireflies.ai
- Otter.ai
- Read AI
- Notion AI

Granola's differentiation includes its no-bot meeting experience, user-led note-taking model, device-based audio capture and increasing emphasis on shared organisational context.

## Funding

In March 2026, Granola announced a **$125 million Series C at a $1.5 billion valuation**, led by Index Ventures, with participation from Kleiner Perkins and existing investors including Lightspeed, Spark and NFDG.

The round provides significant capital for continued product development and commercial expansion.

## Geographic Expansion

Granola was designed and built in London but now describes its customer base as predominantly US-based. It has opened a San Francisco office as the hub for its North American go-to-market organisation.

The simultaneous hiring of US sales, RevOps and Customer Success roles suggests that San Francisco is not simply an engineering or satellite office. It is being built as a commercial operating centre.

## Commercial Signals

Several observable signals appear within the same period:

1. A $125M Series C and $1.5B valuation.
2. Expansion from individual AI notepad toward shared team workspace and organisational context platform.
3. A predominantly US customer base and new San Francisco GTM hub.
4. Simultaneous hiring across SDR, High Velocity AE, Mid-Market AE, Enterprise AE, Customer Success and Revenue Operations.
5. Product capabilities supporting larger organisations, including enterprise security, administration, API and MCP functionality.

Individually, each signal is useful. Together, they suggest a structural commercial transition.

## Working GTM Hypothesis

**Granola appears to be crossing from product-led and prosumer adoption into a deliberately engineered, multi-segment commercial organisation, particularly in the US, while preserving the organic product-led motion that created demand in the first place.**

The emerging structure is visible in the segmentation of its commercial hiring: high-velocity sales to convert product-qualified demand, dedicated mid-market and enterprise motions, Customer Success for retention and expansion, and a first US Revenue Operations leader to build the operational foundation beneath them.

This is therefore better understood as a **commercial infrastructure buildout** than simply "Granola is hiring." The combination of funding, enterprise product development, geographic expansion and complementary GTM roles creates a higher-confidence signal that the company is formalising how it converts organic adoption into repeatable revenue.
